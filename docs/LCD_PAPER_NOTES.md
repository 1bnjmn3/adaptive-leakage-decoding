# Notes on the Local Clustering Decoder paper

Ziad et al., arXiv:2411.10343, read as the ar5iv HTML (header November 2024). v2 (23 Jan 2026) is *Nat. Commun.* 16, 11048 (2025). The abstract and the \(d = 33 \to 17\), 75% and \(1\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) lines match the Nature/v2 text that was visible. The full v2 PDF was not compared line by line (uncertain).

## The “4× fewer physical qubits” claim

Abstract: “Under a realistic circuit-level noise model where leakage is a dominant error source, our decoder enables one million error-free quantum operations with 4x fewer physical qubits when compared to standard non-adaptive decoding.”

Results and Fig. 3(a) attach this to the **high-leakage** fit: \(\Lambda\) from 2.12 (non-adaptive) to 3.85 (adaptive). The target is error probability \(10^{-6}\) on one \(d \times d \times d\) window, “a proxy of a fundamental unit of logic in lattice surgery”. Quote: “Non-adaptive decoding would require \(d = 33\), whereas adaptive decoding requires only \(d = 17\). Since in the surface code the number of physical qubits scales with \(d^{2}\), adaptive decoding enables a near 75% reduction in the number of physical qubits required compared to standard non-adaptive decoding.” The introduction calls the same step “effectively halving” \(d\). With \(2d^{2}-1\) qubits per patch, \((33/17)^{2} \approx 3.8\), the abstract’s “4x”, and the cut is about 73%, their “near 75%”. Curves and FPGA tables stop at \(d = 17\); the paper does not say \(d = 33\) was simulated. Low leakage, same window, moves only \(d = 21\) to \(d = 19\) (\(\Lambda\) 3.23 to 3.95), “an 18% reduction”. The Fig. 3 baseline is non-adaptive LCD. They say the gain takes the decoder “to \(\Lambda^{2}\)”, halving the distance leakage would otherwise cost (Appendix A.3, Fig. 6); the fitted \(2.12 \to 3.85\) is not a numerical square.

## Noise model

Methods IV.6, Table 1: the core “replicates the SI1000” model (Gidney et al. 2021), plus leakage and relaxation. Gates: H, CZ, computational-basis measure and reset. Pauli scale \(p\): CZ depolarising \(p\); one-qubit Clifford and idle \(p/10\); reset and resonator idle \(2p\); measurement flip \(5p\). Low leakage: \(p = 10^{-3}\), \(p_{l} = 10^{-4}\). High leakage: \(p = p_{l} = 5\times 10^{-4}\). The “4x” line is the second of these.

## Leakage model

Stim with a leakage bit per qubit (IV.4). A leakage channel of strength \(p_{l}\) on CZ and on reset sets the bit and replaces the Pauli frame by \(I, X, Y\) or \(Z\) equally. The bit stays set until relaxation or reset; while it is set, a CZ fully depolarises the partner. Relaxation (bit cleared, state left mixed) has strength \(p_{l}\) on CZ, \(p/5\) on one-qubit Cliffords and idle, and \(4p\) on resonator idle. Reset forces \(|0\rangle\) and clears the bit; Table 1 also lists leakage \(p_{l}\) on reset, and the order is not stated (uncertain). Detection is only at measurement: the herald means the qubit leaked at an unknown time since its last reset, with false-negative probability \(5p\). False positives are called much rarer and have no table entry.

LRU: patch wiggling. Data and ancilla roles swap every round, so every qubit resets every two rounds and leakage lasts at most two (introduction, Appendix A.2); hook edges reverse (Fig. 1). Appendix A.1 separates this from erasure: an erasure is mixed at a known location and does not keep damaging neighbours.

## Decoders, target, hardware

LCD is unweighted distributed union-find (edge weight fixed at 2) on a coarse processing-element array (II, IV.1). Each herald pre-grows edges that a leak since the last reset would make likelier (II.2, IV.5). They say pre-growing “has the same effect as reweighting all edges affected by leakage to weight 0”, and that no new edges are added. Fig. 3 is adaptive LCD against non-adaptive LCD; there is no MWPM curve. Weighted Suchara-style updates are later work. The \(10^{-6}\) window is the abstract’s “one million error-free” operations, using \(P_{L} \propto \Lambda^{-d/2}\) (Eq. 1). Fig. 3(a) plots per-round error, 10 million shots per point.

SystemVerilog on a Xilinx VU19P and a ZCU111 (IV.3). “An ASIC implementation of LCD is not explored at this point in time.” At \(d = 17\) the VU19P design uses under 10% of the chip (Fig. 3(c)). Abstract: “decoding in under 1 \(\mu\)s per round”. The introduction says that at \(d = 17\) the decoder is “comfortably within the time budget of 1 \(\mu\mathrm{s}\) per round of syndrome extraction”. Fig. 3(b) (VU19P, 285 MHz) is under that line up to \(d = 17\), adaptivity on or off; time per round is the window time divided by \(d\). The discussion’s latency target “is about \(d\) \(\mu\mathrm{s}\) for an \(O(d)\) window, or 1 \(\mu\mathrm{s}\)/round.” Table 4, using each distance’s maximum clock, lists \(0.930\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) as the slowest VU19P entry (adaptive, high leakage, \(d = 17\)) and \(1.127\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) for that point on the ZCU111. These table and section numbers are v1's. In v2 the timing table is Table 3, with \(0.676\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) (VU19P) and \(0.820\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) (ZCU111) for that point, and the noise table is Table 4 in IV.7.

## Why our comparison is not like-for-like

- **Leakage.** Theirs persists, spreads through later CZs, and dies within two rounds because of wiggling. The herald is a noisy “sometime since reset” flag, and LCD pre-grows a cluster. Ours (`circuits.py`) is rotated Z memory plus `HERALDED_ERASE(p_leak)` on every data qubit at round start and every measure qubit just before measurement: one-shot \(I/X/Y/Z\), perfect herald, no persistence, no spreading, no LRU. That is their Appendix A.1 erasure.
- **Pauli noise.** Theirs is SI1000-shaped at \(p = 10^{-3}\) or \(5\times 10^{-4}\). Ours sets the four Stim probabilities `after_clifford_depolarization`, `before_round_data_depolarization`, `before_measure_flip_probability` and `after_reset_flip_probability` all to \(2\times 10^{-3}\). Our \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}\) is not their per-CZ \(p_{l}\).
- **Decoder.** They compare unweighted LCD on an FPGA. We compare PyMatching on the leaky DEM, heralds ignored; PyMatching on the \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}=0\) DEM with herald edges set to probability \(1/2\) (weight 0), found by a noiseless `FlipSimulator`; and C++ union-find in `hw/` with erasure flags (`INTERFACE.md`), grown as in Delfosse–Zémor. Weight 0 here is one or two edges of a known erasure, inside weighted matching.
- **Saving and timing.** Their 4× is a \(\Lambda\) fit to a \(10^{-6}\) window from curves plotted to \(d = 17\). Ours is \(d = 3, 5, 7\), rounds \(= d\), then the fit in `stats.py`. Their times are FPGA cycles. Our measured values (full run, `results/`):
  - Fitted \(\Lambda\) (static → adaptive) is 5.07 → 5.07 at \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}=0\), 4.35 → 5.03 at \(2\times10^{-3}\), 2.82 → 5.01 at \(10^{-2}\), and 2.09 → 4.52 at \(2\times10^{-2}\).
  - At \(10^{-6}\) per \(d\)-round window, the qubit ratio is 1.23× at \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}=2\times10^{-3}\) (d ≈ 16.8 vs 15.1), 2.57× at \(10^{-2}\) (d ≈ 25.0 vs 15.6), and 4.78× at \(2\times10^{-2}\) (d ≈ 37.0 vs 16.9). These are extrapolated from \(d \le 7\).
  - Decode time per round is CPU only, on one core of an 8-vCPU x86-64 cloud VM ("Intel Xeon Processor"). At \(d=7\), PyMatching `decode_batch` takes 0.53 µs and the C++ union-find (erasure-aware) takes 0.47 µs mean and 3.5 µs worst shot. These are not FPGA latencies.
  - So we reach a ~4× ratio only when \(p_{\mathrm{leak}}\) is ten times our Pauli rate. That is a different regime from their \(p = p_l\), and this is not a reproduction of their result.
